Warren Buffett outlined his most famous plan to fix the U.S. deficit during a CNBC interview, stating:

"I could end the deficit in five minutes. You just pass a law that says that any time there is a deficit of more than 3% of GDP, all sitting members of Congress are ineligible for reelection. Yeah, now you've got the incentives in the right place, right?" 

Buffett's proposal focuses on holding lawmakers personally accountable for fiscal health, suggesting that passing legislation with strict consequences would immediately alter the incentives governing federal spending.

But we need budget surpluses to start whittling down the debt, so this bill goes ever further.

Fiscal Responsibility and Congressional Accountability Act

To amend title 3, United States Code, and the Federal Election Campaign Act of 1971, to establish statutory fiscal accountability for members of Congress by disqualifying sitting members from reelection during periods of excessive federal deficits, and for other purposes.

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the United States of America in Congress assembled,

1. FINDINGS AND PURPOSE

The purpose of this Act is to align the personal electoral incentives of members of Congress with the fiscal health of the United States by establishing immediate political consequences for structural federal deficits.

 

2. RE-ELECTION INELIGIBILITY FOR FISCAL RESPONSIBILITY FAILURE.

(a) Ineligibility Trigger.—Notwithstanding any other provision of law, if the total federal budget deficit for any given fiscal year exceeds the Gross Domestic Product (GDP) of the United States for that same fiscal year, all individuals who served as members of the Senate or the House of Representatives during that fiscal year shall be immediately ineligible to run for re-election or election to any seat in the Senate or the House of Representatives.

(b) Determination of Deficit and GDP

  1. Reporting Agency.—The Director of the Congressional Budget Office (CBO), in consultation with the Bureau of Economic Analysis (BEA), shall issue a final, audited fiscal report within thirty (30) days following the end of each fiscal year.

  2. Certification.—The report must certify the exact federal surplus as a percentage of that fiscal year's nominal GDP.

(c) Enforcement by Election Authorities.—

  1. Federal Election Commission (FEC).—Upon certification that the deficit exceeded the budget, the FEC shall ban affected individuals from registering political committees or raising funds for future congressional campaigns.

  2. State Authorities.—Chief election officers of each State shall refuse to place the name of any ineligible individual on any primary, general, or special election ballot for federal office.

 

3. EXCEPTIONS AND EXEMPTIONS

(a) Declaration of War.—The provisions of Section 3 shall not apply if a formal declaration of war against a foreign nation is in effect pursuant to Article I, Section 8, Clause 11 of the United States Constitution during more than 180 days of the applicable fiscal year.
