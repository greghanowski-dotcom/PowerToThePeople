The Blueprint for a Fairer, Faster Healthcare System: How We Can Cut Costs and Eliminate Wait Times

For decades, Americans have faced a broken healthcare system. We are trapped in a system that demands the highest prices in the world while making us wait weeks or months just to see a doctor.

According to global data from the OECD, the United States spends an astronomical $14,885 per person every year on healthcare. By comparison, our neighbors in Mexico spend just $1,588 per person—over nine times less. Yet, despite spending trillions, we are plagued by confusing medical bills, expensive insurance premiums, and long delays for appointments.

It does not have to be this way. By taking the best ideas from highly efficient healthcare models around the world, we can build a new system designed specifically for the American economy. This blueprint fixes the structural flaws of our current model to deliver low costs, zero deductibles, and short wait times.

The Problem: Why is American Healthcare So Expensive and Slow?

Our current system is built on artificial shortages and immense waste. Four major structural flaws drive up your bills and keep you stuck in the waiting room:

  1. The Paperwork Monster (Administrative Waste): Hospitals and private insurance companies spend nearly 30% of every healthcare dollar fighting each other over complex medical codes, insurance claims, and coverage denials.

  2. Artificial Doctor Shortages: Historical caps on federal funding for doctor training have created an artificial shortage of physicians. This shortage drives specialist salaries up and pushes appointment wait times to an average of over 30 days.

  3. Fear of Lawsuits (Defensive Medicine): To protect themselves from a highly litigious legal culture, American doctors order billions of dollars in redundant, unnecessary tests just to build a protective paper trail. This wastes resources and clogs up diagnostic machines like MRIs.

  4. The Corporate Profit Skim: Private hospital conglomerates and pharmaceutical middlemen operate to maximize profit margins and pay corporate dividends, inflating the price of routine care and life-saving medications.

The Solution: A Hybrid, Low-Cost Premium Utility Model

Our new system transforms healthcare into a streamlined public utility—similar to public schools or the fire department—while preserving consumer choice through a private safety valve.

Pillar 1: Low-Cost, Predictable Monthly Premiums

Instead of expensive private insurance plans with confusing, multi-thousand-dollar deductibles, the system is funded by low-cost monthly premiums tied directly to your income.

  • The Math: Workers pay a predictable 2% to 4% of their monthly income. For an average worker making $50,000, healthcare costs a clean $80 to $160 a month.

  • Deductibles: $0. Deductibles are completely abolished.

  • Co-Pays: A symbolic $5 for a primary doctor and $15 for a specialist. This stops system abuse and overcrowding while remaining low enough to never block care for those in need.

Pillar 2: Vaporizing the Bureaucracy

The government takes over the basic healthcare infrastructure and launches a single, open-source digital billing network. Private insurance companies are wound down. Because the payer and the hospital use the same automated portal, billing paperwork and manual "prior authorizations" disappear.

Pillar 3: Absolute Bulk-Purchasing Power

The government acts as a single buyer, purchasing medical supplies and prescription drugs for all 340 million Americans at once. This massive leverage forces pharmaceutical companies to drop prices to rock-bottom wholesale rates, cutting prescription costs by 50% to 70% instantly.

Pillar 4: Flooding the Doctor Supply

Medical school becomes tuition-free in exchange for public service, allowing doctors to graduate with zero student debt. Federal limits on residency training slots are removed. Additionally, Nurse Practitioners (NPs) and Physician Assistants (PAs) are granted full practice autonomy to handle routine clinical care, breaking specialist bottlenecks and cutting wait times down to days or hours.

Pillar 5: The Regulated Private Safety Valve

To prevent the long wait times seen in other government systems, a heavily regulated, cash-pay private market acts as a traffic buffer:

  • The 70/30 Split: To keep their medical licenses, all doctors must spend at least 70% of their weekly hours treating patients in the public system. They can use the remaining 30% for private clinics.

  • Flat Bundle Pricing: Private clinics are banned from confusing line-item bills. They must publish a single, all-inclusive flat rate for elective procedures (e.g., an MRI or a hip replacement).

  • The Funding Loop: A 15% tax is placed on all private medical transactions. This money goes directly back into the public system to fund advanced equipment and staff, ensuring the private market actively subsidizes shorter wait times for public patients.

The Economics: Savings in Real Dollars

By implementing this architecture, total national healthcare spending will drop by 33% to 43% overall.

The 10-Year Implementation Plan: A Safe Economic Glide Path

Because healthcare represents nearly 18% of the U.S. economy, we cannot change it overnight without causing massive economic disruption. Our transition winds down waste safely across a decade:

  • Years 1 to 3 (The Clean Up): We deploy the unified open-source billing software, launch the low-cost premium brackets, and phase out the private insurance claims bureaucracy.

  • Years 4 to 6 (The Workforce Wave): We implement single-buyer pharmaceutical bulk purchasing and lift federal residency caps. The first wave of debt-free primary care doctors and autonomous NPs enters the workforce, driving down wait times.

  • Years 7 to 10 (System Maturity): Private hospital conglomerates finish paying off legacy corporate debts and transition into non-profit public entities. The 70/30 doctor split matures, locking in our permanent 40% cost reduction and creating a stable, fast healthcare system for generations to come.
